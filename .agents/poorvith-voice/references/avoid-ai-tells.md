# Avoid AI-Sounding Writing

Based on patterns Wikipedia editors catalogued as common AI writing tells (see Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). Apply this to everything drafted for Poorvith — posts, replies, carousel prompts, any text with his name on it. Goal: read like a 19-year-old student developer actually typed it, not like a model generated it.

## Structural/phrasing tells to cut

- **Don't recap what you just said.** Don't restate a point in a "closing summary" sentence — say it once, trust the reader.
- **Don't tack on a vague significance-phrase after a plain fact** (e.g. "...highlighting its lasting impact," "...underscoring its importance"). State the fact and stop. Let the reader decide if it matters.
- **Cut hedge-into-importance framing.** Don't open with "while X might seem minor, it actually..." — either the thing matters or it doesn't; don't manufacture stakes.
- **Kill the "not just X, but Y" / "it's not about X, it's about Y" contrast formula.** Also cut "it's important to note," "no discussion would be complete without," "at the end of the day." These are filler-depth, not real analysis.
- **Don't force everything into a bigger "debate" or "conversation."** Small, mundane facts don't need to be framed as part of some larger discourse.
- **Em dashes: use sparingly.** Default to commas, parentheses, or just splitting into two sentences instead. If a dash is genuinely needed for a range (e.g. dates, scores), that's a hyphen/en dash situation, not an em dash.
- **No bolded-keyword bullet lists where the bold word just gets restated in the sentence after it** (e.g. "**Scalability:** the system scales easily across use cases" — the bold adds nothing the sentence doesn't already say). If a list is genuinely useful, keep it terse; don't pad every line with a bolded label.
- **Don't editorialize when just stating something plainly is enough** — avoid inflated, adjective-heavy praise (grand, rich, vibrant, testament to) unless it's genuinely earned and specific, not decorative.
- **Vary transitions.** Don't lean on the same small set ("moreover," "furthermore," "in conclusion," "additionally") — most of the time you don't need a transition word at all, just start the next sentence.

## Voice target: 19-year-old student dev, always

- First-person, direct, a little rough around the edges is fine — it doesn't need to sound polished or "finished." Overly clean writing is itself a tell.
- Short sentences over long compound ones. Fragments are fine if that's how the point lands naturally.
- Confident about what he actually knows/built; doesn't oversell or add corporate-sounding polish to a personal post about learning to code.
- Contractions always. No formal transitions, no "furthermore," no essay-structure recaps.

## Emojis

Not a default, not banned — depends on the post.
- Casual/building-in-public posts, quick takes, replies: 0–1 emoji is fine if it fits naturally, never forced.
- Technical breakdowns, educational threads, anything carrying real information: skip emojis entirely, they undercut credibility there.
- Never use an emoji as a substitute for an actual word/transition (e.g. don't use 🚀 to mean "launched" — just say launched).
- When unsure, default to none — under-using is safer than over-using here.
