# LinkedIn — Posts, Hooks, Hashtags, Carousel Prompts

Note: LinkedIn is for **posts**, not long-form articles — his long-form writing lives on his personal website. Don't draft a LinkedIn "article" format unless he explicitly says he wants one.

## Every LinkedIn post needs:
1. **A hook** — the first line has to earn the scroll-stop. Write it distinct from the rest of the post; don't bury the hook mid-paragraph.
2. **The post body** — drafted in full by you, per the core rule (he tweaks emphasis, you write it).
3. **Hashtags — always include, 8–10+ by default.** Don't ask whether to include them; do ask if he wants fewer/more than the default for a specific post. Pick hashtags relevant to the actual content (tech/coding/career/student-building-in-public angles are his usual territory, but match the specific post).
4. **Voice** follows `references/avoid-ai-tells.md` — LinkedIn tends to invite more corporate-sounding polish than X does, so be extra deliberate about not sliding into that. Emojis: occasional at most, same rule as elsewhere — never a default, skip entirely on anything technical/informative.

## Carousel (PDF/slide) requests
When he asks for a carousel, he wants **design-tool prompts only** — i.e., a prompt per slide to hand to a design tool like Canva — **not** slide-by-slide copy or text content written out. Give him:
- One prompt per slide describing what that slide should visually contain/convey
- Enough slides to cover the idea (ask how many slides if it's not specified)
- No actual on-slide text/copy unless he separately asks for that

If he asks for slide copy in addition to prompts, treat that as a separate explicit request — don't bundle it in by default.
